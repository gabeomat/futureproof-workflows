# Meeting follow-up

Write a follow-up email from a meeting transcript. The email has the same structure every time, states only what was said in the meeting, and says plainly that it was drafted with AI.

The person sending this email will be judged by it. Write it so they can send it with no edits.

## What you need from the person

Ask only for what is missing:

- **The transcript.** Pasted text, or a file path. Recording tools like Zoom, Fathom, Otter, or Wispr Flow all export one.
- **Who it's going to.** First name(s) of the recipient(s).
- **The sender's name** for the sign-off.

Do not ask anything else. Work out the date, topics, decisions and next steps from the transcript.

## The email: use this structure exactly

```
Subject: Summary and next steps from our call on [Day, Month Date]

Hi [first name],

Great call today. Here's a summary of what we covered and the next steps.

What we covered
- [One factual line per topic]

Decisions
- [One line per decision]

Next steps
- [Owner]: [action] (by [date])

Have a great week. If you have questions, let me know.

[Sender name]

This summary was drafted with AI from our call transcript.
```

Structure rules:

- The greeting, the opening line, the closing line and the AI note are fixed. Use them word for word.
- **The date:** use the call date only if the transcript or the person states it. Otherwise write `[call date]` in the subject and list it under "Check before sending". Never work out a weekday yourself.
- If the email goes out on a later day, change "Great call today." to "Great call on [day]." and nothing else.
- **What we covered:** 3 to 7 bullets, in the order they came up.
- **Decisions:** only choices the people in the meeting clearly agreed on. A task someone will do goes in Next steps, never here. Tentative talk ("I'm thinking", "maybe", "probably") is not a decision; it goes in What we covered. If nothing was decided, delete the whole section, heading included.
- In What we covered and Decisions, call the recipient "you" and the sender "I" or "we". In Next steps, start each bullet with the owner's first name.
- The AI note is the last line inside the email block, under the sender's name.
- **Next steps:** one bullet per action, starting with the person who owns it. Add "(by [date])" only when a date was said out loud.
- Every bullet is one line, 20 words or fewer.
- Nothing goes in the email outside this structure: no P.S., no extra paragraph, no added resources.

## Write only the facts

Everything in the email must come from the transcript. Do not add anything the people in the meeting did not say.

Never write:

- **Praise or kudos.** No "great progress", "exactly the right move", "you're on the right track", "love where this is going".
- **Coaching or advice** that wasn't given in the meeting. No tips, reminders, encouragement or "one thing to keep in mind".
- **Interpretation.** Report what was said, not what it means or why it matters.
- **Warm-up or filler.** No "I hope this finds you well", "It was a pleasure", "Thanks so much for your time", "Just wanted to recap".
- **Imitations of the sender's personality.** No jokes, catchphrases, emojis or exclamation points.
- **Em dashes.** Use a period or comma instead.

Use the words people used in the meeting. Don't swap in fancier ones or add adjectives.

An example of what to avoid in the opening:

> "We tackled your pricing page today, and honestly that was exactly the right place to start!"

The fixed opening line replaces all of that:

> "Great call today. Here's a summary of what we covered and the next steps."

## Keep out of the email

Leave these out, and list them in the check list below instead:

- Personal or sensitive things mentioned in passing (health, family, money trouble, complaints about other people), unless they are an action item.
- Anything said "off the record" or "between us".
- Prices, figures or dates you can't confirm from the transcript word for word.

## What to hand back

1. **The email**, in one copy-ready block, exactly in the structure above.
2. **Check before sending**, a short list outside the email, covering:
   - next steps with no clear owner or date
   - anything the transcript was unclear on (crosstalk, cut-off sentences, names you weren't sure how to spell)
   - anything you left out on purpose, and why

   If there is nothing to check, write "Nothing to check."

## Before you hand it back

Read the email once more and fix it if any of these are true:

- [ ] The opening, closing or AI note differs from the fixed wording.
- [ ] Any line praises, encourages, advises or interprets.
- [ ] Any fact, name, number or date isn't in the transcript.
- [ ] A section is there with nothing real in it.
- [ ] A bullet runs past one line.
- [ ] There's an em dash, emoji or exclamation point.
